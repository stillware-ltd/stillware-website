# Service providers: the household licence service (RankUp Chess)

**Status: in force (written 25 October 2026, KL.8). Every provider's terms are accepted: the last, Turso's DPA, was signed by the founder on 28 October 2026 (F2).** The COPPA Rule (16 CFR 312.8(c), as amended 22 April 2025) asks an operator to take reasonable steps to release children's personal information only to service providers that can keep it confidential and secure, and to get their written assurance that they will. "Email plus" also depends on there being no disclosure to third parties: every provider below acts only for the service. Nothing is sent to anyone else.

Each DPA link was found on 25 October 2026. Read the current text in the provider's dashboard when you accept it: the version you accept is the one that counts.

## Questions for the outside check (KL.8e)

1. **Paddle is the seller, not only a processor.** For its own buyers it is a controller of their data. The sign-in sends Paddle an address a parent typed, to find a purchase Paddle already holds. Is that a disclosure to a third party under the Rule, or support for internal operations? The plan names it as the open question (section 10).
2. **Turso:** settled. The service moved to the Developer plan and requested Turso's DPA (25 October 2026); the founder signed it on 28 October 2026.
3. **Where each provider keeps the data** (US, EU, UK), for the privacy policy and for UK GDPR transfers. Turso: its DPA says its primary processing takes place in the United States (section 5.1), and its trust page says backups go to AWS S3 in us-east-2 whatever the database's region (read 28 October 2026). The database's own region is still to be read in the Turso dashboard.
