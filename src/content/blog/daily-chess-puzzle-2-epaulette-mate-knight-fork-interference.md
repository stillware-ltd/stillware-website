## Puzzle 1: Epaulette mate (beginner, rated 1037)

![Puzzle 1: White to play after Re8](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-2-epaulette-mate-knight-fork-interference/puzzle-1.webp)

**White to play.** Black has just played rook to e8. The black king on d8 is short of squares. Can you find the checkmate?

### Solution

1. **Rd7+** — White: Rook to d7, check.
2. **Bxd7** — Black: Bishop takes on d7.
3. **Rxd7#** — White: Rook takes on d7, checkmate.

Rook to d7, check. We offer the rook, and the king cannot take it, because our rook on h7 protects it. Black has no choice. Bishop takes on d7. Now, rook takes on d7, checkmate.

![Puzzle 1 final position after Rxd7#](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-2-epaulette-mate-knight-fork-interference/solution-1.webp)

**The pattern.** An epaulette mate. The king is flanked by its own rooks, like epaulettes on a uniform, so a check from straight in front, by a queen or a rook, is the end. Your opponent's own pieces can be the bars of the cage.

## Puzzle 2: Knight fork (intermediate, rated 1338)

![Puzzle 2: White to play after c6](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-2-epaulette-mate-knight-fork-interference/puzzle-2.webp)

**White to play.** Black has just played pawn to c6. Notice the black king on e8 and the black queen on f5, close together. Where can our knight land to attack both at once?

### Solution

1. **Nd6+** — White: Knight to d6, check.
2. **Kd7** — Black: King to d7.
3. **Nxf5** — White: Knight takes on f5.

Knight to d6, check. The knight attacks the king on e8 and the queen on f5 at the same time. Black has to move the king. King to d7. And now, knight takes on f5. That wins the queen.

![Puzzle 2 final position after Nxf5](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-2-epaulette-mate-knight-fork-interference/solution-2.webp)

**The pattern.** We win the queen for a knight. A knight fork. One knight, one jump, two targets, and your opponent can save only one of them. Before every move, check whether your knight is a single jump from the king and another heavy piece.

## Puzzle 3: Interference (advanced, rated 1931)

![Puzzle 3: Black to play after Be2](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-2-epaulette-mate-knight-fork-interference/puzzle-3.webp)

**Black to play.** White has just played bishop to e2. Notice the white king on g1 and the white queen on d1, close together. Two of White's pieces are protecting each other. Can you find the move that cuts the line between them?

### Solution

1. **Bc2** — Black: Bishop to c2.
2. **Qa1** — White: Queen to a1.
3. **Bxa4** — Black: Bishop takes on a4.

Bishop to c2. The bishop attacks the queen on d1 and the knight on a4 at the same time. White defends with queen to a1. Bishop takes on a4. That wins the knight.

![Puzzle 3 final position after Bxa4](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-2-epaulette-mate-knight-fork-interference/solution-3.webp)

**The pattern.** We come out a piece ahead. Interference. We put a piece between two enemy pieces that were protecting each other, and the connection is cut. When two defenders share a line, look for the move that gets in between them.
