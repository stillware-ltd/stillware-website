## What happened

At dinner, someone asks Dev how he affords living alone. He says it is only £550 more. Year one actually cost him £9,000. Dev is twenty-nine, a software engineer taking home £6,100 a month, and the gap between those two figures is worth understanding before any move up.

His room in a flat-share cost £1,150 a month. The flat he now lives in alone costs £1,700. The difference is £550 a month, and that is the number he says when anyone asks. It is accurate, and it is the only figure anyone ever quotes.

Over twelve months, though, that step comes to £6,600, and nobody tends to say the year. Then there is the setup. A flat-share arrives with a sofa, a kettle, a hoover, plates and the internet already in place. A flat on your own arrives with a floor. Dev needed a sofa, a bed, kitchen things, curtains and a desk, along with the difference in the deposit. That came to £2,400, all of it spent in the first two months.

Put together, his first year living alone cost £9,000. Spread honestly over the year, that is £750 a month, not £550.

That is the turn. "Only £550 more" was true, and it was also useless. The real decision was a £9,000 one, and it had been made using the smaller number. The monthly step was easy to accept because it sounded modest; the setup never featured in the thinking at all, because it was a one-off rather than a rent payment.

Any move up, whether to living alone, a bigger place or a nicer one, has the same two costs. There is the step, multiplied by twelve, and there is the setup. Add them together, divide by twelve, and that is the honest monthly figure for the first year. After that, the setup is paid and only the step remains.

So the honest answer at dinner sounds slightly different. It is £750 a month for a year, then £550 after that. It is a real answer, and it tends to end the conversation rather than invite a second question. Dev's setup costs now sit in their own envelope beside the new rent line, so his first year has a number on it, £750 a month, rather than the smaller one he used to say.
