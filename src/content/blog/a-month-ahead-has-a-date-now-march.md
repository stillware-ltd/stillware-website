Day one, Dev divided his take-home by what he saves and got fifteen months, and closed the spreadsheet. Day thirty, someone told him a month ahead is a month of the bills that do not move: £2,080, five months, March.

**Dev, twenty-nine, software engineer.** The households in these Shorts are illustrations built from typical UK figures, not real people, and every number below is worked on the household's sheet.

## What happened

On day one, Dev decided he wanted to get a month ahead, so that each month's bills would be paid from money that had already arrived. He is twenty-nine, a software engineer, and he takes home £6,100 a month. He sat down with a spreadsheet and did the sum that seemed obvious. He took his take-home pay and divided it by what he manages to save each month, which is £420. The answer was fifteen months. He closed the spreadsheet, because more than a year felt like the kind of goal that quietly gets abandoned.

On day thirty, someone told him the thing nobody tends to say out loud. A month ahead is not a month of income. It is a month of the bills that do not move. For Dev that meant two lines. Rent is £1,700 a month. Bills are £380. Together they come to £2,080, and nothing more is needed. The groceries, the evenings out and the rest of the spending still come out of each month's pay as normal; what needs to be sitting there in advance is only the money that has to leave on a fixed date.

At £420 a month, £2,080 takes five months to save. Starting in October, the goal is full by the end of February, and Dev is a month ahead from March. Nothing about his income or his saving had changed between day one and day thirty. That is the turn. The number did not move; the question did. How much he earned had become how much had to leave, and the second question has a much smaller answer.

There was a shortcut available too. A single share vest of £4,000 would cover nearly two months of fixed costs in one step. But the shortcut is not really the point. The point is that the goal now has a date on the calendar instead of sitting under the heading of someday.

What a month ahead buys is easy to describe. On the first of each month, next month's rent and bills are already there. Payday stops being a deadline and becomes simply the day money arrives. The plan itself fits on three lines: fixed costs on one, saving on another, and the division between them. Then the month goes in the calendar. Dev's goal already holds his first £420 and points to February, so from March the rent no longer waits on payday.
