## Puzzle 1: Pillsbury's mate (beginner, rated 1097)

![Puzzle 1: Black to play after e8=Q](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-16-pillsbury-s-mate-discovered-check-sacrifice-queen-fork/puzzle-1.webp)

**Black to play.** White just played pawn to e8, promoting to a queen. The white king on g1 is short of squares. The king is running out of squares. Can you find the winning check?

### Solution

1. **Ra1+** — Black: Rook to a1, check.
2. **Rb1** — White: Rook to b1.
3. **Rxb1#** — Black: Rook takes on b1, checkmate.

Rook to a1, check. The king cannot go to f1, because our rook on a1 controls that square. White's only move is rook to b1. And now, rook takes on b1, checkmate.

![Puzzle 1 final position after Rxb1#](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-16-pillsbury-s-mate-discovered-check-sacrifice-queen-fork/solution-1.webp)

**The pattern.** Pillsbury's mate: a rook on the open g-file and a bishop on the long diagonal aim at the castled king together. Open the file, and the bishop does the rest.

## Puzzle 2: Discovered check (intermediate, rated 1391)

![Puzzle 2: White to play after gxh6](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-16-pillsbury-s-mate-discovered-check-sacrifice-queen-fork/puzzle-2.webp)

**White to play.** Black has just played pawn takes on h6. It wins a bishop, or so it seems. Notice the black king on g8 and the black queen on d8, close together. There is a check here that changes everything. Can you find it?

### Solution

1. **Nxe6+** — White: Knight takes on e6, check.
2. **Kh8** — Black: King to h8.
3. **Nxd8** — White: Knight takes on d8.

Knight takes on e6, check. The knight steps aside, and our rook on g1 now looks straight at the black king on g8. Black steps out of check. King to h8. Now, knight takes on d8. That wins the queen for our knight.

![Puzzle 2 final position after Nxd8](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-16-pillsbury-s-mate-discovered-check-sacrifice-queen-fork/solution-2.webp)

**The pattern.** A discovered check. If you were tempted by knight takes on f7, check, Black answers king takes on f7 and the chance is gone.

## Puzzle 3: Sacrifice (advanced, rated 2049)

![Puzzle 3: Black to play after Qxe4](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-16-pillsbury-s-mate-discovered-check-sacrifice-queen-fork/puzzle-3.webp)

**Black to play.** White has just played queen takes on e4. It wins a pawn, or so it seems. The white king on g2 is short of squares. There is a forcing sequence here, and it starts with a sacrifice.

### Solution

1. **Qf2+** — Black: Queen to f2, check.
2. **Rxf2** — White: Rook takes on f2.
3. **R8xf2#** — Black: Rook from 8 takes on f2, checkmate.

Queen to f2, check. We offer the queen, and the king cannot take it, because our rook on f1 protects it. White has no choice. Rook takes on f2. And now, rook from 8 takes on f2, checkmate.

![Puzzle 3 final position after R8xf2#](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-16-pillsbury-s-mate-discovered-check-sacrifice-queen-fork/solution-3.webp)

**The pattern.** A sacrifice is not a gift. We give up material because what comes next is worth more: a mate, a winning attack, or the material back with interest.

## Puzzle 4: Queen fork (master, rated 2672)

![Puzzle 4: Black to play after Qg4](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-16-pillsbury-s-mate-discovered-check-sacrifice-queen-fork/puzzle-4.webp)

**Black to play.** White just played queen to g4. Notice the white king on g2 and the white queen on g4, close together. Where can our queen land to attack both at once?

### Solution

1. **Qc2+** — Black: Queen to c2, check.
2. **Kh3** — White: King to h3.
3. **f5** — Black: Pawn to f5.
4. **Qg6** — White: Queen to g6.
5. **fxe4** — Black: Pawn takes on e4.

Queen to c2, check. The queen attacks the king on g2 and the rook on e4 at the same time. White steps out of check. King to h3. And now, pawn to f5. The pawn attacks the queen on g4 and the rook on e4 at the same time. White plays queen to g6. Pawn takes on e4. That wins the rook.

![Puzzle 4 final position after fxe4](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-16-pillsbury-s-mate-discovered-check-sacrifice-queen-fork/solution-4.webp)

**The pattern.** A queen fork. The queen attacks two undefended targets at once, often with check, and only one of them can be saved.
