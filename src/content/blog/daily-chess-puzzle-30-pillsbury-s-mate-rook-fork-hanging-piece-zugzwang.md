## Puzzle 1: Pillsbury's mate (beginner, rated 1027)

![Puzzle 1: Black to play after Bxh6](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-30-pillsbury-s-mate-rook-fork-hanging-piece-zugzwang/puzzle-1.webp)

**Black to play.** White just played bishop takes on h6. It wins a queen, or so it seems. The white king on g1 is short of squares. The king is running out of squares. Can you find the winning check?

Play it on Lichess before reading on: [lichess.org/training/01Hgu](https://lichess.org/training/01Hgu). It was rated 1027 in the Lichess database we took it from; ratings there shift a little as people solve it.

### Solution

1. **Rg6+** — Black: Rook to g6, check.
2. **Bg5** — White: Bishop to g5.
3. **Rxg5#** — Black: Rook takes on g5, checkmate.

Rook to g6, check. The rook attacks the king on g1 and the bishop on h6 at the same time. White's only move is bishop to g5. Now, rook takes on g5, checkmate.

![Puzzle 1 final position after Rxg5#](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-30-pillsbury-s-mate-rook-fork-hanging-piece-zugzwang/solution-1.webp)

**The pattern.** Pillsbury's mate: a rook on the open g-file and a bishop on the long diagonal aim at the castled king together. Open the file, and the bishop does the rest.

## Puzzle 2: Rook fork (intermediate, rated 1623)

![Puzzle 2: Black to play after dxe4](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-30-pillsbury-s-mate-rook-fork-hanging-piece-zugzwang/puzzle-2.webp)

**Black to play.** White has just played pawn takes on e4. It wins a pawn, or so it seems. And the white bishop on c2 has nothing defending it. The winning move gives up material. Can you see why it works?

Play it on Lichess before reading on: [lichess.org/training/01acW](https://lichess.org/training/01acW). It was rated 1623 in the Lichess database we took it from; ratings there shift a little as people solve it.

### Solution

1. **Rd2+** — Black: Rook to d2, check.
2. **Rxd2** — White: Rook takes on d2.
3. **Rxd2+** — Black: Rook takes on d2, check.
4. **Kf3** — White: King to f3.
5. **Rxc2** — Black: Rook takes on c2.

Rook to d2, check. We offer the rook, and the king cannot take it, because our rook on d8 protects it. White takes. Rook takes on d2. And now, rook takes on d2, check. The rook attacks the king on e2 and the bishop on c2 at the same time. White has to move the king. King to f3. Rook takes on c2. That wins the bishop.

![Puzzle 2 final position after Rxc2](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-30-pillsbury-s-mate-rook-fork-hanging-piece-zugzwang/solution-2.webp)

**The pattern.** A rook fork. If you were tempted by pawn takes on e4, White answers rook takes on d4 and the chance is gone.

## Puzzle 3: Hanging piece (advanced, rated 2292)

![Puzzle 3: White to play after Rf8](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-30-pillsbury-s-mate-rook-fork-hanging-piece-zugzwang/puzzle-3.webp)

**White to play.** Black just played rook to f8. And the black bishop on e6 has nothing defending it. There is a check here that changes everything. Can you find it?

Play it on Lichess before reading on: [lichess.org/training/02AGl](https://lichess.org/training/02AGl). It was rated 2292 in the Lichess database we took it from; ratings there shift a little as people solve it.

### Solution

1. **Qxe6+** — White: Queen takes on e6, check.
2. **Kh7** — Black: King to h7.
3. **Rg1** — White: Rook to g1.

Queen takes on e6, check. The king cannot go to f7, because our queen on e6 controls that square. Black steps out of check. King to h7. And now, rook to g1.

![Puzzle 3 final position after Rg1](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-30-pillsbury-s-mate-rook-fork-hanging-piece-zugzwang/solution-3.webp)

**The pattern.** We come out a piece ahead. A hanging piece: undefended, and free to take. If you were tempted by queen takes on f8, check, Black answers queen takes on f8 and the chance is gone.

## Puzzle 4: Zugzwang (master, rated 2579)

![Puzzle 4: White to play after Kf3](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-30-pillsbury-s-mate-rook-fork-hanging-piece-zugzwang/puzzle-4.webp)

**White to play.** Black just played king to f3. It looks natural, but it leaves something behind. No checks, no captures. Can you find the move that leaves Black with nothing good to play?

Play it on Lichess before reading on: [lichess.org/training/019vo](https://lichess.org/training/019vo). It was rated 2579 in the Lichess database we took it from; ratings there shift a little as people solve it.

### Solution

1. **h6** — White: Pawn to h6.
2. **gxh6** — Black: Pawn takes on h6.
3. **gxh6** — White: Pawn takes on h6.
4. **Kg3** — Black: King to g3.
5. **Kg5** — White: King to g5.

Pawn to h6. Black takes. Pawn takes on h6. Now, pawn takes on h6. That wins the pawn. Black tries king to g3. King to g5.

![Puzzle 4 final position after Kg5](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-30-pillsbury-s-mate-rook-fork-hanging-piece-zugzwang/solution-4.webp)

**The pattern.** Zugzwang. Every move your opponent has makes their position worse, and they have to move.
