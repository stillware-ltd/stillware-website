## Puzzle 1: Back-rank mate (beginner, rated 849)

![Puzzle 1: White to play after Qg3](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-4-back-rank-mate-deflection-double-check/puzzle-1.webp)

**White to play.** Black has just played queen to g3. Take a moment to look at the whole board. Would you give up your queen here? There is a reason to.

### Solution

1. **Qxf7+** — White: Queen takes on f7, check.
2. **Kh8** — Black: King to h8.
3. **Qf8+** — White: Queen to f8, check.
4. **Rxf8** — Black: Rook takes on f8.
5. **Rxf8#** — White: Rook takes on f8, checkmate.

Queen takes on f7, check. We give up the queen for a pawn. Black's only move is king to h8. Now, queen to f8, check. We offer the queen. So Black has just one move: rook takes on f8. Rook takes on f8, checkmate.

![Puzzle 1 final position after Rxf8#](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-4-back-rank-mate-deflection-double-check/solution-1.webp)

**The pattern.** A back-rank mate. The king's own pawns become its prison, and one heavy piece on the last rank ends the game. Before you castle and forget about it, ask whether your back rank has an escape square.

## Puzzle 2: Deflection (intermediate, rated 1594)

![Puzzle 2: Black to play after Rg4](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-4-back-rank-mate-deflection-double-check/puzzle-2.webp)

**Black to play.** White just played rook to g4. It looks natural, but it leaves something behind. One defender is doing all the work. Can you find the move that drags it away?

### Solution

1. **Rxe5+** — Black: Rook takes on e5, check.
2. **Kxe5** — White: King takes on e5.
3. **Kxg4** — Black: King takes on g4.

Rook takes on e5, check. We give up the rook for a pawn. White has to take it. King takes on e5. King takes on g4. That wins the rook.

![Puzzle 2 final position after Kxg4](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-4-back-rank-mate-deflection-double-check/solution-2.webp)

**The pattern.** We come out a pawn ahead. Deflection. A defender is doing an important job, so we force it to leave, and the thing it was defending falls. Ask what each defender is guarding, then find the move that drags it away.

## Puzzle 3: Double check (advanced, rated 2117)

![Puzzle 3: Black to play after Bxf5](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-4-back-rank-mate-deflection-double-check/puzzle-3.webp)

**Black to play.** White has just played bishop takes on f5. It wins a knight, or so it seems. Where can our knight land to attack both at once?

### Solution

1. **Nxb3+** — Black: Knight takes on b3, check.
2. **Kb1** — White: King to b1.
3. **Ra1+** — Black: Rook to a1, check.
4. **Bxa1** — White: Bishop takes on a1.
5. **Nxd2+** — Black: Knight takes on d2, check.

Knight takes on b3, check. The knight attacks the king on c1 and the knight on d2 at the same time. White steps out of check. King to b1. And now, rook to a1, check. We offer the rook, and the king cannot take it, because our knight on b3 protects it. So White has just one move: bishop takes on a1. Knight takes on d2, check. The knight steps aside, and our queen on b6 now looks straight at the white king on b1.

![Puzzle 3 final position after Nxd2+](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-4-back-rank-mate-deflection-double-check/solution-3.webp)

**The pattern.** A double check. Two pieces give check at once, so the only answer is a king move: nothing can be captured or blocked. Whenever a piece can move with check and uncover another, look at it twice. If you were tempted by pawn takes on f5, White answers king to b1 and the chance is gone.
