## Puzzle 1: Mate in three (beginner, rated 985)

![Puzzle 1: Black to play after Rd8](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-29-mate-in-three-exposed-king-deflection-sacrifice/puzzle-1.webp)

**Black to play.** White has just played rook to d8. It looks safe. It is not. Two targets, one move. Can you find the fork?

Play it on Lichess before reading on: [lichess.org/training/00Gv1](https://lichess.org/training/00Gv1). It was rated 985 in the Lichess database we took it from; ratings there shift a little as people solve it.

### Solution

1. **Re1+** — Black: Rook to e1, check.
2. **Rxe1** — White: Rook takes on e1.
3. **Rxe1+** — Black: Rook takes on e1, check.
4. **Kf2** — White: King to f2.
5. **Qf1#** — Black: Queen to f1, checkmate.

Rook to e1, check. The rook attacks the king on g1 and the rook on d1 at the same time. White takes. Rook takes on e1. Now, rook takes on e1, check. The king cannot go to f1, because our queen on h3 controls that square. White has no choice. King to f2. Queen to f1, checkmate.

![Puzzle 1 final position after Qf1#](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-29-mate-in-three-exposed-king-deflection-sacrifice/solution-1.webp)

**The pattern.** A mate in three. Every move is a check, so the opponent never gets a free move to defend.

## Puzzle 2: Exposed king (intermediate, rated 1667)

![Puzzle 2: White to play after Kh8](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-29-mate-in-three-exposed-king-deflection-sacrifice/puzzle-2.webp)

**White to play.** Black has just played king to h8. Take a moment to look at the whole board. The first move is a check. Which piece gives it?

Play it on Lichess before reading on: [lichess.org/training/02AXB](https://lichess.org/training/02AXB). It was rated 1667 in the Lichess database we took it from; ratings there shift a little as people solve it.

### Solution

1. **Qh6+** — White: Queen to h6, check.
2. **Kg8** — Black: King to g8.
3. **Qf8+** — White: Queen to f8, check.
4. **Kh7** — Black: King to h7.
5. **Ng5#** — White: Knight to g5, checkmate.

Queen to h6, check. The king cannot go to g7, because our queen on h6 controls that square. Black steps out of check. King to g8. Now, queen to f8, check. We offer the queen, and the king cannot take it, because our knight on e6 protects it. So Black has just one move: king to h7. Knight to g5, checkmate.

![Puzzle 2 final position after Ng5#](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-29-mate-in-three-exposed-king-deflection-sacrifice/solution-2.webp)

**The pattern.** An exposed king. Once the pawn shelter is gone, checks come from everywhere and the defence cannot keep up.

## Puzzle 3: Deflection (advanced, rated 2240)

![Puzzle 3: White to play after Qxe4](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-29-mate-in-three-exposed-king-deflection-sacrifice/puzzle-3.webp)

**White to play.** Black just played queen takes on e4. It wins a pawn, or so it seems. And the black bishop on g3 has nothing defending it. One defender is doing all the work. Can you find the move that drags it away?

Play it on Lichess before reading on: [lichess.org/training/01i0h](https://lichess.org/training/01i0h). It was rated 2240 in the Lichess database we took it from; ratings there shift a little as people solve it.

### Solution

1. **Nh5** — White: Knight to h5.
2. **Be5** — Black: Bishop to e5.
3. **Qg5+** — White: Queen to g5, check.
4. **Qg6** — Black: Queen to g6.
5. **Qxe5** — White: Queen takes on e5.

Knight to h5. Black plays bishop to e5. And now, queen to g5, check. The queen attacks the king on g8 and the bishop on e5 at the same time. Black tries queen to g6. And now, queen takes on e5. That wins the bishop.

![Puzzle 3 final position after Qxe5](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-29-mate-in-three-exposed-king-deflection-sacrifice/solution-3.webp)

**The pattern.** Deflection. A defender is doing an important job, so we force it to leave, and the thing it was defending falls.

## Puzzle 4: Sacrifice (master, rated 2424)

![Puzzle 4: Black to play after Qxd5](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-29-mate-in-three-exposed-king-deflection-sacrifice/puzzle-4.webp)

**Black to play.** White has just played queen takes on d5. It wins a pawn, or so it seems. The white king on h1 is short of squares. The first move is a check. Which piece gives it?

Play it on Lichess before reading on: [lichess.org/training/02YEp](https://lichess.org/training/02YEp). It was rated 2424 in the Lichess database we took it from; ratings there shift a little as people solve it.

### Solution

1. **Ng3+** — Black: Knight to g3, check.
2. **Kh2** — White: King to h2.
3. **Rxh3+** — Black: Rook takes on h3, check.
4. **gxh3** — White: Pawn takes on h3.
5. **Qf2#** — Black: Queen to f2, checkmate.

Knight to g3, check. The king cannot go to g1, because our queen on c5 controls that square. So White has just one move: king to h2. And now, rook takes on h3, check. We give up the rook for a pawn. So White has just one move: pawn takes on h3. Queen to f2, checkmate.

![Puzzle 4 final position after Qf2#](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-29-mate-in-three-exposed-king-deflection-sacrifice/solution-4.webp)

**The pattern.** A sacrifice is not a gift. We give up material because what comes next is worth more: a mate, a winning attack, or the material back with interest.
