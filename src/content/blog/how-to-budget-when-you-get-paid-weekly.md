**The short answer: if you are paid weekly, one week in every month is asked for more than twice what it pays. On a household taking home £1,280 a month, the week the rent leaves is £324.62 short — and nothing you do inside that week can fix it. The money has to come from the other three, £228.46 at a time.**

## The standard advice is right, and it is not the problem

Search *how to budget when you get paid weekly* and you get the same two conversions everywhere, from banks and budgeting sites alike:

- weekly take-home × 52 ÷ 12 = what you actually earn in a month
- monthly bills × 12 ÷ 52 = what each weekly packet owes

Both are correct. So is the warning that comes with them — four weekly pay days cover 28 days, and most months are 30 or 31, so treating four packets as a month's income leaves you short every month. That is good advice and this article is not going to argue with it.

The problem is what the advice stops short of. It tells you how to convert the numbers. It does not tell you **which week breaks**, or by how much, and that is the week everybody paid weekly already knows about.

## One household, one month, in pounds

Everything below is one worked household — the same one used in the video above and in the rest of this series. Take-home is £1,280 a month, paid weekly.

| | |
|---|---:|
| Monthly take-home | £1,280.00 |
| **Paid weekly** (1,280 × 12 ÷ 52) | **£295.38 a week** |
| Rent | £620.00 |
| Council tax | £105.00 |
| Energy | £95.00 |
| Water | £32.00 |
| Phone | £38.00 |
| This month's dated one-offs (school lunches, car insurance, a birthday) | £100.00 |
| **Everything with a date on it** | **£990.00 a month** |
| Groceries and transport | £325.00 a month |

Four packets land in most months and they are all the same size: £295.38. One of those weeks has the rent in it.

## The rent week is £324.62 short

Rent is £620.00. The pay that week is £295.38.

**£620.00 − £295.38 = £324.62 short.**

That is not a spending problem and it is not a discipline problem. That week was never given the money. You can order it, prioritise it, cut everything discretionary out of it and you are still £324.62 down, because the sum does not work.

This is the number the standard advice leaves out. It converts your income and it converts your bills, and then it hands you four equal packets and a bill that is bigger than any one of them.

## Why "split each week's pay across that week's bills" runs out

The usual method — take each week's pay, cover the bills due that week, carry the rest forward — works for three weeks out of four. In those weeks the bills are small, the packet covers them, and the remainder rolls on.

It cannot work for the fourth. No ordering of £295.38 makes it cover £620.00. A method is not wrong for having a limit; it just has one, and this is it.

## The fix: every packet pays a share of everything dated

Instead of asking each week to cover the bills that happen to fall in it, ask each week to pay **the same share of the whole month**.

Add up everything with a date on it for the month — £990.00 here — take a year of that, and split it evenly across every packet in that year:

**£990.00 × 12 ÷ 52 = £228.46 a week.**

Set that aside the day the money lands, before anything else. Do it for four weeks and the rent envelope holds £572.31 of the £620.00 by the next rent day — not quite the whole rent, and the last £47.69 is what the fifth packet is for (more on that below).

The rent week stops being a cliff because it is no longer being asked to pay the rent on its own.

## What is actually left: £66.92 a week

£295.38 lands. £228.46 is spoken for. What remains is:

**£295.38 − £228.46 = £66.92.**

That is the only number that matters in a normal week. Not the account balance, which still has other weeks' set-aside sitting in it, and not what landed on Friday.

And this is where the household finds out something the monthly version of this budget hides.

## The £8.08 nobody sees

Groceries and transport come to £325.00 a month for this household. Across a year of weeks that is:

**£325.00 × 12 ÷ 52 = £75.00 a week.**

The plan wants £75.00. The money is £66.92.

**£75.00 − £66.92 = £8.08 short, every week.**

Take that gap across a year of weeks and back into months and it is **£35.00** — exactly what this household's monthly budget shows as a shortfall on day one, before a single purchase. (Take it from the rounded £8.08 instead and you get £35.01; the penny is rounding, and the underlying figures are £75.00 against £66.9231.) Weekly pay did not create that gap and weekly pay cannot hide it. It just arrives in pieces small enough to ignore.

That is the real advantage of doing it this way. Eight pounds a week is a size you can answer: a cheaper shop, one subscription dropped, fewer journeys. The same money, discovered at a card machine after it has piled up for four weeks, is a much worse conversation.

## "But I am starting this week, and the rent is Friday"

Nothing above helps the rent that is due on Friday. You cannot set aside a month of shares in three days, and no guide should pretend otherwise.

The first cycle is the one that hurts. Start the set-aside **the week after the rent leaves**, and by the next rent day the envelope holds a month of shares. One hard month takes the cliff out of every month after it.

If rent or council tax are already behind, that is a different problem and it comes first — talk to [StepChange](https://www.stepchange.org/) or [Citizens Advice](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/debt-and-money/) before you budget anything.

## The fifth packet is not a bonus

Four times a year, five pay days land in the same month. It looks like free money and it is not.

A week's share is sized for **every** packet in the year: 52 × £228.46 is £11,879.92 against 12 × £990.00 = £11,880.00, and the eight pence is rounding — taken to the exact share they are the same figure. Which means the eight four-packet months each take in about £76.15 less than they owe, and the four five-packet months each take in about £152.31 more. Eight of the first exactly cancel four of the second: £609.23 either way.

The fifth packets are the balancing item. Spend one as a bonus and the other months start coming up short again. (The last £47.69 of that first rent envelope comes from here too.)

## The method, in five steps

1. Write down what actually lands in a normal week — net pay, not your hourly rate. If your hours vary, average the last 8 to 12 weeks and budget from the lower end.
2. List everything with a date on it for the **month**: rent, council tax, energy, water, phone, and this month's one-offs.
3. Add it up, multiply by 12, divide by 52. That is the share every weekly packet owes.
4. Set that share aside the day the money lands, before anything else.
5. What is left is the week. If it is under what the week needs, you have found your shortfall at its smallest — answer it there.

The set-aside does not need a second bank account. It needs to be a figure you stop counting as yours the moment it lands, which is the whole job an envelope does, on paper or on a screen. Moving it somewhere else is optional. Deciding it is not yours is not.

## Frequently asked

**How do I budget monthly bills when I get paid weekly?**
Add up everything with a date on it for the month (£990 here), multiply by 12 and divide by 52, and set
that share (£228.46) aside from every packet the day it lands. The rent week stops being a cliff because
no single week is asked to pay the rent on its own.

**What is the best way to budget when you get paid weekly?**
Fund the month's dated bills by the share above, then budget only what is left (£66.92 a week here) for
groceries and transport. If that is short, you have found the month's real shortfall at its smallest
(£8.08 a week here) and can answer it where it is small.

**Does this work if I am paid every two weeks?**
Yes, with 26 packets instead of 52: monthly dated bills × 12 ÷ 26 is the share each fortnightly packet
owes. The rent-week gap is smaller because each packet is bigger, but it is still there in any month
where the rent lands.

**What should I do with the fifth pay day in a five-week month?**
Treat it as the balancing item, not a bonus. The weekly share is sized for 52 packets, so the four
five-packet months are what pay for the eight four-packet months. Spend one and the other months come
up short again.
