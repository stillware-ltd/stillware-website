## The short version

Both apps teach the same method: **zero-based (envelope) budgeting**, where every dollar gets a job before the month begins. The method works. The difference is everything around it:

## Where YNAB is better

An honest comparison has to start here.

**Automatic bank sync.** YNAB connects to thousands of banks and pulls transactions in automatically. Zeroed deliberately doesn't — no bank logins, no Plaid, no third-party aggregators. If typing or importing your transactions is a dealbreaker, YNAB wins this one outright. (We'd argue manual awareness is half the point of budgeting — but that's a philosophy, not a fact.)

**A web app.** YNAB runs in any browser. Zeroed is installed software; there's no web version, because a web version would mean our servers holding your data.

**Shared budgets.** YNAB Together lets partners work from one budget across accounts. Zeroed households share a device or a synced Drive folder — workable, but not purpose-built collaboration.

**Community and education.** YNAB has spent a decade building courses, a subreddit, and a fandom. That ecosystem has real value when you're learning the method.

## Where Zeroed is better

**You pay once.** YNAB's price has climbed from $50/year to $109/year over the years, and every renewal is a new decision. Zeroed is a single purchase — $19.99 at the founder price — and [version 1.x is yours forever](/zeroed). Over five years that's roughly **$525 kept in your pocket**, which is a strange thing for a budgeting app to make you spend.

**Your financial data never leaves your device.** This is the architectural difference everything else flows from. YNAB's model requires their servers to hold your transactions. Zeroed's can't — there are no servers. Data is AES-256 encrypted on your device, and optional sync goes through *your own* Google Drive, encrypted before it leaves. There is no Zeroed account, no email address attached to your finances, nothing for anyone to breach.

**It works completely offline.** On a plane, in a dead zone, or just because you prefer it — every feature works without internet.

**Imports that don't break.** Bank-linking APIs disconnect, re-authenticate, and miss transactions. Zeroed's universal parser reads CSV, PDF, OFX, QFX, and QIF statements from any bank — the formats banks have exported reliably for decades — with automatic duplicate detection. There's also an OCR receipt scanner for cash spending.

**No hostage data.** If your trial ends and you never pay, Zeroed switches to read-only: you can still open it, see everything, and export it all. We think that's how software should treat you.

## The price math, plainly

## Which should you choose?

**Choose YNAB if** you need automatic bank sync, budget jointly with a partner in real time, want a web app, or lean on its community and courses. It's good software with a rental price tag.

**Choose Zeroed if** you want your budget to be private, offline, and permanently yours — and you'd rather pay once than rent. Especially if you've watched YNAB's price climb and thought: *a budgeting app should not be a recurring line in my budget.*
