## What happened

Leah's room costs £1,030 a month. When anyone asks, she says £780. She is thirty-one, a civil servant taking home £2,600 a month, and the difference between those two figures shapes a decision she is about to make.

Working out the real cost takes three numbers. The first is the rent, £780, which is the only one anyone ever says. It is easy to quote because it is a single fixed payment, but on its own it is not the cost of living there.

The second number is the bills that exist only because she lives in that home. Her share of those comes to £210 a month. The third is the stuff a home quietly eats: toilet roll, light bulbs, cleaning products. It comes to about £40 a month, and nobody ever counts it, because it arrives in small amounts mixed in with the weekly shop.

Added together, the three come to £1,030, not £780. That is the turn: the number she says is not the number she pays. Home costs roughly a third more than rent once the bills and the household line are counted, and only one of those three numbers ever gets spoken.

It matters now because she is looking at a flat for £950 a month, and she has been comparing that figure with £780. Rent against rent. That comparison flatters the room and makes the flat look like a bigger jump than it may be in some respects, while hiding costs in others. The honest comparison is home against home, and a flat lived in alone tends to eat more than a room in a shared house, with no one to split the bills or the cleaning cupboard with.

The method takes about three minutes. Take the rent, add a share of the bills, then find the household line on a recent statement and add that too. The total is what home actually costs. For Leah it works out at around 40 percent of what she takes home. That is a perfectly reasonable share, as long as it is the number that sits in the plan rather than the smaller one she quotes. Her rent, bills and household spending now sit as three rows under one heading called Home, so when the flat is weighed up it will be weighed against £1,030, not £780.
