## Puzzle 1: Opera mate (beginner, rated 996)

![Puzzle 1: White to play after Kxh7](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-7-opera-mate-zugzwang-hanging-piece/puzzle-1.webp)

**White to play.** Black just played king takes on h7. It wins a queen, or so it seems. The king is running out of squares. Can you find the winning check?

### Solution

1. **Rh3+** — White: Rook to h3, check.
2. **Kg8** — Black: King to g8.
3. **Rh8#** — White: Rook to h8, checkmate.

Rook to h3, check. The king cannot go to h6, because our rook on h3 controls that square. Black has no choice. King to g8. And now, rook to h8, checkmate.

![Puzzle 1 final position after Rh8#](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-7-opera-mate-zugzwang-hanging-piece/solution-1.webp)

**The pattern.** The opera mate. A rook on the back rank, protected by a bishop on the long diagonal, and the king's own pawn blocking the escape. Rook and bishop, working on two lines at once. If you were tempted by bishop takes on e5, Black answers knight takes on e5 and the chance is gone.

## Puzzle 2: Zugzwang (intermediate, rated 1392)

![Puzzle 2: Black to play after Kxa4](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-7-opera-mate-zugzwang-hanging-piece/puzzle-2.webp)

**Black to play.** White has just played king takes on a4. It wins a pawn, or so it seems. No checks, no captures. Can you find the move that leaves White with nothing good to play?

### Solution

1. **Kb6** — Black: King to b6.
2. **Ka3** — White: King to a3.
3. **Kb5** — Black: King to b5.
4. **Ka2** — White: King to a2.
5. **Ka4** — Black: King to a4.

King to b6. White defends with king to a3. And now, king to b5. White plays king to a2. King to a4.

![Puzzle 2 final position after Ka4](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-7-opera-mate-zugzwang-hanging-piece/solution-2.webp)

**The pattern.** Zugzwang. Every move your opponent has makes their position worse, and they have to move. In the endgame, having no good move is as bad as being attacked.

## Puzzle 3: Hanging piece (advanced, rated 2097)

![Puzzle 3: Black to play after R8c5](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-7-opera-mate-zugzwang-hanging-piece/puzzle-3.webp)

**Black to play.** White just played rook from 8 to c5. And the white queen on g8 has nothing defending it. There is a capture that your opponent cannot answer. Can you find it?

### Solution

1. **Rxg8** — Black: Rook takes on g8.
2. **Rd1+** — White: Rook to d1, check.
3. **Qd6** — Black: Queen to d6.
4. **Rxd6+** — White: Rook takes on d6, check.
5. **Kxd6** — Black: King takes on d6.

Rook takes on g8. That wins the queen. White plays rook to d1, check. Queen to d6. We offer the queen. White takes. Rook takes on d6, check. And now, king takes on d6. That wins the rook.

![Puzzle 3 final position after Kxd6](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-7-opera-mate-zugzwang-hanging-piece/solution-3.webp)

**The pattern.** We come out a rook ahead. A hanging piece: undefended, and free to take. Before every move, look at every piece on the board once and ask whether it is protected. If you were tempted by rook takes on c5, White answers rook to d1, check and the chance is gone.
