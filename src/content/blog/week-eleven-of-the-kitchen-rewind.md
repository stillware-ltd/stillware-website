## What happened

It is week eleven of the kitchen, and the builder wants the third stage payment by Friday. The bill says £2,400. The quote said £2,000. To see how that happened, the story has to go back to the spring.

Grace and Femi Okafor have two teenagers and take home £9,800 a month. In March, a bonus paid for the new kitchen: £6,000, set aside in one go. It felt like a cash purchase, money already in hand and the job as good as paid. It was not quite that. The builder's terms split the £6,000 into three stage payments of £2,000 each: one at the start, one at first fix, and one at the end.

Then the job did what jobs do. In week four, the tiles the couple had chosen turned out not to be the tiles in the quote. In week seven, it was the extractor. In week nine, a socket had to move. Each change was small, and each one arrived as a sensible thing to sort while the walls were open anyway. Together they came to £850 of extras, taking the kitchen from £6,000 to £6,850. Part of that landed on the final invoice, so the third stage was no longer £2,000 but £2,400, and it fell due in the month with nothing spare.

The turn is that the budget did not break. The calendar did. The money was set aside in March as one number, but the bill arrived in three parts spread over the weeks, and the last one was the biggest. Every project carries a margin of around fifteen per cent that nobody quotes. On this kitchen that was £900, enough to cover the £850 of extras. Had it been added to the quote before a single wall came down, each extra would have been a transfer from an envelope rather than an argument at the kitchen table.

The second change is about dates. Put on the calendar, each stage payment has a day attached, and the last one stops being a surprise in the month the household can least afford one. For the next project, the Okafors' approach is set: the quote plus fifteen per cent goes into an envelope, the stages are written down with their dates, and the biggest payment is funded first, so that the £2,400 is visible long before the Friday it is due.
