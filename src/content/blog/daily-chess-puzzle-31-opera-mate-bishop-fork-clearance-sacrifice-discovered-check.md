## Puzzle 1: Opera mate (beginner, rated 1035)

![Puzzle 1: Black to play after h5](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-31-opera-mate-bishop-fork-clearance-sacrifice-discovered-check/puzzle-1.webp)

**Black to play.** White has just played pawn to h5. The white king on g3 is short of squares. Notice how close our rook on f4 and our bishop on g1 are to the white king on g3. Can you find the checkmate?

Play it on Lichess before reading on: [lichess.org/training/01Dsa](https://lichess.org/training/01Dsa). It was rated 1035 in the Lichess database we took it from; ratings there shift a little as people solve it.

### Solution

1. **Bf2+** — Black: Bishop to f2, check.
2. **Kh3** — White: King to h3.
3. **Rh4#** — Black: Rook to h4, checkmate.

Bishop to f2, check. We offer the bishop, and the king cannot take it, because our rook on f4 protects it. The king must move. King to h3. Now, rook to h4, checkmate.

![Puzzle 1 final position after Rh4#](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-31-opera-mate-bishop-fork-clearance-sacrifice-discovered-check/solution-1.webp)

**The pattern.** The opera mate. A rook on the back rank, protected by a bishop on the long diagonal, and the king's own pawn blocking the escape.

## Puzzle 2: Bishop fork (intermediate, rated 1442)

![Puzzle 2: White to play after Nxd3](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-31-opera-mate-bishop-fork-clearance-sacrifice-discovered-check/puzzle-2.webp)

**White to play.** Black has just played knight takes on d3. It wins a bishop, or so it seems. And the black knight on d3 has nothing defending it. There is a forcing sequence here, and it starts with a sacrifice.

Play it on Lichess before reading on: [lichess.org/training/01EEp](https://lichess.org/training/01EEp). It was rated 1442 in the Lichess database we took it from; ratings there shift a little as people solve it.

### Solution

1. **Bxg7+** — White: Bishop takes on g7, check.
2. **Kh7** — Black: King to h7.
3. **Bxf8** — White: Bishop takes on f8.
4. **Bxf8** — Black: Bishop takes on f8.
5. **cxd3** — White: Pawn takes on d3.

Bishop takes on g7, check. We give up the bishop for a pawn. Black has to move the king. King to h7. And now, bishop takes on f8. That wins the rook for our bishop. Black takes back. Bishop takes on f8. Pawn takes on d3. That wins the knight.

![Puzzle 2 final position after cxd3](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-31-opera-mate-bishop-fork-clearance-sacrifice-discovered-check/solution-2.webp)

**The pattern.** A bishop fork. Two targets on crossing diagonals, one bishop between them. If you were tempted by knight takes on g7, Black answers bishop to c5 and the chance is gone.

## Puzzle 3: Clearance sacrifice (advanced, rated 2065)

![Puzzle 3: Black to play after Nc4](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-31-opera-mate-bishop-fork-clearance-sacrifice-discovered-check/puzzle-3.webp)

**Black to play.** White has just played knight to c4. The white king on c1 is short of squares. The winning move gives up material. Can you see why it works?

Play it on Lichess before reading on: [lichess.org/training/01hsz](https://lichess.org/training/01hsz). It was rated 2065 in the Lichess database we took it from; ratings there shift a little as people solve it.

### Solution

1. **Ne2+** — Black: Knight to e2, check.
2. **Bxe2** — White: Bishop takes on e2.
3. **Qa1#** — Black: Queen to a1, checkmate.

Knight to e2, check. We offer the knight. White's only move is bishop takes on e2. And now, queen to a1, checkmate.

![Puzzle 3 final position after Qa1#](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-31-opera-mate-bishop-fork-clearance-sacrifice-discovered-check/solution-3.webp)

**The pattern.** A clearance sacrifice. A piece gets out of the way, with check or with a threat, so that another piece can use the square or the line it leaves behind.

## Puzzle 4: Discovered check (master, rated 2718)

![Puzzle 4: White to play after Kf8](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-31-opera-mate-bishop-fork-clearance-sacrifice-discovered-check/puzzle-4.webp)

**White to play.** Black has just played king to f8. It looks natural, but it leaves something behind. Would you give up your knight here? There is a reason to.

Play it on Lichess before reading on: [lichess.org/training/0H4SK](https://lichess.org/training/0H4SK). It was rated 2718 in the Lichess database we took it from; ratings there shift a little as people solve it.

### Solution

1. **Ne5** — White: Knight to e5.
2. **dxe5** — Black: Pawn takes on e5.
3. **fxe5+** — White: Pawn takes on e5, check.
4. **Bf6** — Black: Bishop to f6.
5. **exf6** — White: Pawn takes on f6.

Knight to e5. We offer the knight. Black takes. Pawn takes on e5. Now, pawn takes on e5, check. The pawn steps aside, and our rook on f1 now looks straight at the black king on f8. Black tries bishop to f6. Pawn takes on f6. That wins the bishop.

![Puzzle 4 final position after exf6](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-31-opera-mate-bishop-fork-clearance-sacrifice-discovered-check/solution-4.webp)

**The pattern.** A discovered check. The piece that moves is not the one giving check; it steps aside and the piece behind it does.
