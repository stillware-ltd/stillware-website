## Puzzle 1: Mate in two (beginner, rated 1034)

![Puzzle 1: White to play after Kf8](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-3-mate-in-two-discovered-attack-knight-fork/puzzle-1.webp)

**White to play.** Black has just played king to f8. The black king on f8 is short of squares. The king is running out of squares. Can you find the winning check?

### Solution

1. **Rd8+** — White: Rook to d8, check.
2. **Ne8** — Black: Knight to e8.
3. **Rxe8#** — White: Rook takes on e8, checkmate.

Rook to d8, check. The rook attacks the king on f8 and the bishop on c8 at the same time. So Black has just one move: knight to e8. Now, rook takes on e8, checkmate.

![Puzzle 1 final position after Rxe8#](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-3-mate-in-two-discovered-attack-knight-fork/solution-1.webp)

**The pattern.** A mate in two. The first move takes away the escape, the second delivers mate, and the reply in between is forced. Forcing moves first, and the mate appears. If you were tempted by knight takes on e5, Black answers bishop to f5 and the chance is gone.

## Puzzle 2: Discovered attack (intermediate, rated 1661)

![Puzzle 2: Black to play after Qf3](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-3-mate-in-two-discovered-attack-knight-fork/puzzle-2.webp)

**Black to play.** White just played queen to f3. And the white rook on a7 has nothing defending it. Notice the white king on g1 and the white queen on f3, close together. Which capture wins material?

### Solution

1. **Bxc6** — Black: Bishop takes on c6.
2. **Qxc6** — White: Queen takes on c6.
3. **Qxa7** — Black: Queen takes on a7.

Bishop takes on c6. The bishop steps aside, and our queen on e7 now looks straight at the white rook on a7. White takes. Queen takes on c6. Queen takes on a7. That wins the rook.

![Puzzle 2 final position after Qxa7](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-3-mate-in-two-discovered-attack-knight-fork/solution-2.webp)

**The pattern.** We come out a rook ahead. A discovered attack. One move creates two threats: the piece that moves attacks something, and the piece it uncovers attacks something else. Your opponent can only meet one of them.

## Puzzle 3: Knight fork (advanced, rated 2135)

![Puzzle 3: White to play after Bb4](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-3-mate-in-two-discovered-attack-knight-fork/puzzle-3.webp)

**White to play.** Black has just played bishop to b4. It looks safe. It is not. Where can our knight land to attack both at once?

### Solution

1. **Nxd4** — White: Knight takes on d4.
2. **Nxd4** — Black: Knight takes on d4.
3. **Qa4+** — White: Queen to a4, check.
4. **Nc6** — Black: Knight to c6.
5. **Bxf5** — White: Bishop takes on f5.

Knight takes on d4. The knight attacks the bishop on f5 and the knight on c6 at the same time. Black takes. Knight takes on d4. And now, queen to a4, check. The queen attacks the king on e8 and the bishop on b4 at the same time. Black tries knight to c6. Now, bishop takes on f5. That wins the bishop.

![Puzzle 3 final position after Bxf5](/blog/images/daily-chess-puzzle-3-mate-in-two-discovered-attack-knight-fork/solution-3.webp)

**The pattern.** We come out a piece ahead. A knight fork. One knight, one jump, two targets, and your opponent can save only one of them. Before every move, check whether your knight is a single jump from the king and another heavy piece. If you were tempted by bishop takes on f5, Black answers knight takes on f5 and the chance is gone.
